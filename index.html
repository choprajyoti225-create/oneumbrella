A Short Romance

One Umbrella

Every Thursday for a year, Clara had watched the man in the green scarf leave The Corner Bookery with a new book of poems and no umbrella, and every Thursday she had told herself that tonight she would finally say hello. Then came the evening the sky broke open just as he stepped onto the cobblestones, and before her nerves could stop her, she was beside him, tilting her umbrella over his head and laughing at how soaked he already was. He told her his name was Elias, that he always forgot umbrellas because he was always reading on the walk home, and that he had noticed her too — the woman who lingered by the window pretending to browse cookbooks. They walked slowly, much more slowly than the rain deserved, sharing the small dry circle between them while the streetlamps blurred gold in the puddles, and by the time they reached the end of the lane neither of them wanted to find shelter anywhere else. He never did start carrying an umbrella; he said he didn't need one anymore, because now there was always room under hers.
